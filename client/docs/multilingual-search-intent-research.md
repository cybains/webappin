# Multilingual keyword and search intent research

Research date: 11 November 2026. Markets: English, German (Germany and Austria), Czech, Polish and Romanian. Primary objective: qualified employer enquiries; secondary objective: relevant job-seeker enquiries.

## Findings and limits

Use explicit recruitment-service language for employers. The distinction between hiring workers from abroad and looking for work abroad matters more than translating “cross-border mobility” literally. Driver searches are mixed: recruitment services, transport-company vacancies and job boards appear together. Add the employer audience to the title and first screen.

This is qualitative public-search research, not a keyword-volume study or a geographically controlled Google ranking audit. Priorities below reflect commercial relevance and observed intent, not measured search demand. Search Console evidence could not be retrieved: the connected GSC Wizard account returned `payment_required` because its trial/subscription is inactive. No monthly volumes, difficulty scores or conversion forecasts have been invented.

Provider pages establish local commercial terminology, not the truth of their marketing claims or Sufoniq's ability to deliver an equivalent service. Proposed phrases below are keyword families to test, not all individually verified search queries. Language coverage does not establish delivery capability in every country.

## Austrian campaign

German is the clearest fit for outreach to Austrian farm decision-makers. Keep the current German title: it already identifies workers, agriculture and Austria. Supporting families include `Saisonarbeitskräfte Landwirtschaft Österreich` and `Erntehelfer Vermittlung Österreich`; permit terminology belongs in useful process answers, not as the main sales offer.

The Czech, Polish and Romanian employer titles above are strategic long-tail proposals, not demonstrated high-volume opportunities. Broad `praca sezonowa Austria`, `muncă sezonieră Austria` and `práce v Rakousku` searches lead toward candidate vacancies. A translated employer campaign remains useful for sharing and referrals, but should not pretend to be a vacancy listing.

## Copy and journey priorities

1. Employer page: say who hires whom, what the employer receives and what to send first. Use one natural local recruitment phrase in the opening; explain screening, candidate interest and the practical preparation in concrete terms.
2. Driver page: identify transport employers immediately. Separate experience screening from licence, professional qualification and permission requirements. Explain the first employer deliverable before asking for the enquiry. Do not imply immediate availability or completed EU credentials.
3. Austria page: lead with staffing requirements, season and agricultural activity. Make the service enquiry distinct from applying for a farm job.
4. Candidate page: promise the support actually provided. Broad “jobs abroad” traffic expects vacancies; offer a clear support journey rather than presenting a non-existent job board.
5. Internal links: link employer service to both specialist offers with descriptive native anchors. Keep candidate links clearly labelled. About and Method & Trust support trust rather than competing for the main service keyword.
6. Insights: match each article to its actual question. The Lithuania quota article, for example, serves rules/research intent; its next step can point to employer help. Do not multiply near-identical country pages without distinct useful information.

Polish needs particular care: `z zagranicy` means workers coming from abroad; `za granicą` means abroad and commonly signals a candidate looking for work. Romanian `angajăm șoferi` reads as a direct job advertisement, whereas `recrutare șoferi pentru companii de transport` makes the service audience clearer.

“Global mobility” can attract tax, payroll, relocation and employer-of-record expectations. It can describe part of the broader story, but should not replace recruitment as the main commercial search target unless those services are actually offered.

## Measurement after implementation

Record a baseline before changing titles, then examine settled Search Console data by page, query and country. Separate branded searches, employer-service phrases and candidate-job phrases. Measure qualified enquiries alongside clicks: more candidate traffic is not evidence of employer acquisition.

Use Poland, Romania, Czechia, Germany and Austria as reporting segments, without assuming every visitor in each country uses its main language. Review impressions and query relevance first when counts are small; avoid attributing a few clicks to a title change. Search volume and controlled local SERP validation remain unmeasured in this research.
